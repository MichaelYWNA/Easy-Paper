# Academic Integrity

Easy-Paper is built for evidence-guided original writing.

## Allowed

Easy-Paper can help you:

- understand how strong papers organize an argument;
- identify what kind of evidence your paragraph needs;
- decide whether a citation supports a specific sentence;
- revise your own claims so they match your data and methods;
- improve clarity, logic, scope and LaTeX correctness;
- record citation provenance and remaining risk.

## Not Allowed

Easy-Paper should not be used to:

- copy a reference paper's sentences and replace words with synonyms;
- stitch together paragraphs from multiple papers;
- hide plagiarism by paraphrasing;
- import another paper's data, figures, methods or results as your own;
- create fake references, fake DOIs or fake BibTeX entries;
- make unsupported claims sound well-cited.

## How Reference Papers Are Used

A reference paper may provide:

- a writing move;
- a section structure;
- a type of evidence;
- a way to state limitations;
- a comparison pattern;
- a citation-worthy claim, if the source really supports it.

A reference paper may not provide:

- final wording for your manuscript;
- unverified claims;
- uncredited data;
- a shortcut around reading and understanding the source.

## Citation Rule

Every citation must answer two questions:

1. Does this source exist, and is its metadata correct?
2. Does this source support the exact claim being made?

If either answer is uncertain, the manuscript should use a weaker claim, mark the issue in the review file, or wait for better evidence.

## AI Disclosure

Some journals and universities require disclosure of AI-assisted writing or editing. Easy-Paper cannot decide institutional policy for you. When preparing a submission, check the target venue's current rules and disclose AI assistance when required.
